# Learning Process

## Way of Thinking

You can't learn everything in the information security field. Pick a path and specialize in it.
Also, you don't need to master something in order to continue learning. Use what you need in the current moment and learn about new stuff as you need it.

There is something called "passive" learning and "active" learning.
Examples of passive learning: only reading the course material.
Examples of active learning: doing exercises and practicing with the information you learn through the course.

**Important:** We have to learn how to *find, choose,* and *adapt* the information we need. The crucial missing term is **LEARN.**

**Important:** The process of *learning how to learn* is very important.

## Think Outside the Box

**Important:** The way of thinking **"Outside the box"** is essential to being a good hacker.

## Occam's Razor

*The most straightforward theory is preferable to all others of several sufficient possible explanations for the same state of facts. In other words: The simplest explanation is always the most probable.*

### Occam's Razor in Practice

*The simplest explanation for the best approach to penetration testing is that we work with the information we can get.*

*The art, after all, is not to get some flag but to find the way to it.*

## Learning Types

### Passive Learning

It only has an effectiveness rate of about 10%-30%.

### Active Learning

It has an effectiveness rate of 50%-75%.

Active learning consists of discussing our entire process, results and findings with other people. That way we can see different points of view and results to compare with our own.

**Important:** Information has a certain level of *quality,* therefore not all information is useful. Furthermore, some information can confuse us and disorient us completely. To discern which information is useful and which is not, we need to develop a *repertoire* and we do that by *practicing.*

In order to stay *motivated,* we must recognize even the most minor of successes. That way, we will notice our own *progress.*

*Progress is noticeable when the question that tortured us has lost its meaning.*

**Important:** Take breaks in order to not burn out and allow the information you learn to have an effect. Otherwise, you will get stuck.

## Questioning

**Important:** Asking the right questions is a critical skill to have.

### Question States

There are no "good" or "bad" questions.

Actual question states:

- *Rough* questions
- *Precise* questions

### Questions in General

*Questions are an essential part of the thinking process in which links are created between information nodes in our brain. Thus, it is also a fixed and unavoidable part of the learning process. Removing questions, therefore, also reduces the learning process enormously.*

Every question is built on three aspects:

1. Origin
2. Process
3. Result/Goal

*A question is a sentence worded or expressed to elicit information.*

*A sentence is a set of words that is complete in itself, typically containing a subject and predicate, conveying a statement, question, exclamation, or command, and consisting of a main clause and sometimes one or more subordinate clauses.*

### Relationship-Oriented-Questioning Model

Components:

1. Your Position
2. The Object
3. Known
4. Unknown
5. Other Position(s)

### Practice

After 5 to 10 times of using the model, we will start using it subconsciously.

*A right question is a precise question that allows us to establish the relationships between the components, to understand them, and to take us one step further to the required answer.*
